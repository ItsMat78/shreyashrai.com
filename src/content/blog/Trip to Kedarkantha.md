One bucket list item has been crossed off and I have conquered the peak of a mountain! 
After a long period of frustrating events and tiring days, the trip came to an end the moment we reached the peak of Kedarkantha, a mountain in the Shivalik Hills, Uttarakhand with an elevation of 12,500 ft (3810 meters). 
But this trek was the latter part of the trip, the first few days were spent sight-seeing Haridwar, Rishikesh, and Mussoorie. And I understood so many things about myself, my friends, and the world.

Let's start chronologically, with all the events I can remember which are either related to me or were relevant enough.

# Day 1
The people in the first train to Delhi were two groups of 8 (ours) and 6 members respectively.

Everyone had trekking bags or these luggage bags, while I had my normal school bag overfilled with stuff. I hadn't packed much the night before, well I had only packed for the actual trek, not knowing we'd be travelling to many more places and I'd feel like shit not having clothes.

### A warm welcome
The receptionist was welcoming and had a memorable presence, which added to the hostel's pleasant first impression.

### Moustache Hostel
The hostel rooms were divided between two separate buildings. Six of our eight beds were in one wing, so all eight of us decided to stay there. The bunk beds were huge, the room was beautiful and spacious, and the mattresses were fluffier than clouds. Yes, this was gold.

After settling in, we rented two scooters and two motorcycles and got ready to explore Rishikesh at night.

### Freedom Cafe
With a Snickers in my mouth, we headed outside on two scooters and two motorcycles. I drove a scooter with R.C. sitting behind me. The path across Laxman Jhula felt barely wider than my handlebars; the slightest deviation seemed capable of sending us off the bridge. We made it through the narrow lanes to Freedom Cafe, a cliffside cafe overlooking the Ganga, where some negotiation and help from a waiter secured us a beautiful seat at the edge of a balcony. 

After the disappointing food, we moved down to the riverbank, where people sat together as they do along the ghats in Varanasi. One man played guitar, couples gathered around, and another couple sat on a rock in the river. I had just sat down when I felt sharp pains inside my shirt. There was a huge insect trapped inside it. My attempts to crush or remove it failed, so P.B. helped hold it while I took off my shirt and threw the insect away. I shamefully put my shirt back on as the people behind me watched.

P.B. sat alone on a rock beside a couple, creating such an unintentionally dramatic scene that S.J. immediately turned it into a meme—then went and joined him. I eventually sat there too and took in the riverbank atmosphere.

### Biking
Afterward, we grabbed our scooters and rode around without a destination. We stopped near a crossing that led toward a dark, forested area, where everyone took turns trying the motorcycles.

Everyone was taking pictures with the motorcycles. When I tried to take one for a ride, S.Y. asked me to wait while he finished his photos. I eventually sat down, assuming I would not get a turn. S.Y. noticed, though, and insisted that I get on; A.S. and the others joined him. After riding for a while, I handed the motorcycle to P.B., who wanted to demonstrate his “driving skills.” He sped along the cliffside for a solid minute. I was scared as fuck, but at least we did not fall.

When we were done with that point, we stopped briefly at a market before driving onward. It was late at night and Laxman Jhula was closed, so Google Maps sent us along a suspiciously long alternate route. Seeing the others leave in the same direction, we followed them.

S.J. and I became philosophical about how one river had shaped an entire city. We drove through complete darkness, surrounded by cliffs, forests, and gravel. Huge trucks passed us, so S.J. took it slowly.

I kept recording the scenery. When we got to the site of boarding the boats, the dudes gave us the equipment. Helmets, life jackets, and oars. But I didn't get an oar :(

### Danger
Seeing another boat's passengers get into the water a second time, we urged our guide to allow us too. After some negotiation, he agreed and we went down. 

Soon after, the wind began to blow, sweeping the boat toward the rocks. The guide panicked, and so did we. As the rocks drew closer, he pulled people back aboard one by one, shouting, *"You should have listened to me!"*

After R.C., S.S., and P.B. were aboard, only S.Y. and I remained in the water. We tried to reach the safe side, but the rotating boat kept bringing us back toward the rocks. P.B. grabbed both our arms at once, smiling despite being unable to pull either of us up. For a moment, I felt completely done for.

The others eventually hauled us aboard. The guide had pulled P.B. up so forcefully that his leg hurt, but everyone was finally out of danger.
Somehow, both of us got pulled up by the others, and S.Y. and I were both pulled onto the boat, out of any danger. 

Breathing and listening to the guide ramble for a few minutes, we went back in the water afterwards too lmao.

A few rapids later, tired arms and one urgent bathroom request ended our run early. After a long negotiation, we paid ₹1,800 for the GoPro pictures and took the operator's jeep back to the hostel.

## Mussoorie

While part of the group searched for a stay, the rest of us watched Dehradun's lights from sunset point. From that height, the city looked like it was being viewed from a plane.

# Day 7

## Trek begins
We were to get up at 8 am, to pack up and get ready for the trek at 10 am. We packed up our stuff in one singular room, put all our clothes in the freaky green bag that we hated, wore all of the gear and left for the trek at about 11 am. Yes we were late lmao.

It was still quite warm in Sankari, and the jackets were proving too warm for us. So before we even got to the first tea point, our jackets were off. 

At the first tea point, N.S. advised us to grab items here itself as it gets expensive up there. Everyone went ham on chocolates, and our bill amounted to ₹1800 through chocolates alone.

## Juda-ka-Talab

  <figcaption>Juda-ka-Talab camp</figcaption>

Exhausted, we reached our first checkpoint: Juda-ka-Talab. Two trekking groups were camped there. Ours had three sleeping tents and one kitchen tent; the other group occupied more than fifteen tents higher up the hill.

We unpacked our sleeping bags and attempted an afternoon nap. Mine contained a mysterious, foul-smelling cloth, so sleep did not come easily. A longer rest, tea, popcorn, and a game of bluff eventually revived me.

As darkness fell, even walking into the woods became intimidating. We crowded into one tent, traded stories, and teased one another about our love lives until everyone became too tired to continue—conveniently just before they reached mine.
I removed the smelly cloth from my sleeping bag, but the night was bitterly cold. By 5 am, I was shivering despite curling tightly into the bag and trying everything I could to warm up.

# Day 9
## The night of the walk to the summit
We woke at 2:10 am, already packed, and waited for tea and breakfast. Mouthwash replaced brushing. Loaded for the night, we ventured out. K. advised us to carry ponchos; with those and the filled water bottles, my bag became fucking heavy. Before I could reconsider anything, the four-kilometre summit trek had begun.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/Trip to Kedarkantha/Pasted image 20260406224700.png" alt="The moonlight and the snow cliffs" width="" height="">
  <figcaption>The moonlight and the snow cliffs</figcaption>
</figure>

The darkness limited us to the person ahead and the patch of ground beneath our headlamps. The route was steeper and longer than anything before it, and exhaustion came quickly. We took turns carrying the heavy bag and climbed slowly. Gaiters and metal spikes gave us traction; without them, slipping on the continuous snow would have been unavoidable.

<figure class="img-right">

The first steep slopes were terrifying. Looking sideways revealed only an endless sheet of snow falling away beneath us. One slip could have been fatal. No joke.
People kept motivating and advising on what was coming and what to do. The poles that we kept using all trek were game-changers and helped in gaining friction. 

At one point, N.S. got scared of the black-ice formation (extra slippery ice due to melting) and asked us to stop. He didn't want us to continue to the peak. But we urged him since coming this far would have meant nothing. I agree we should be listening to our guide, but like come on. Don't steal my summit from me.

At the first tea stall, we rested, switched bags, and ate chocolates. S.J. and I climbed together, sharing water and snacks, until S.Y. caught up. R.C., as usual, was somehow far ahead.

As we had started late (we actually began at 4 am) the sun started to make the sky brighter. It felt as if we'd never get to see the sunrise from the peak. This motivated us to go up faster. But slow steps were taken.

At the second and final tea stall, N.S. was at his limit. He had a severe headache and decided that he would stop and wait for us to go up. We took the opportunity and left our raincoats with him at the tea-stall. This made things easier. 

Closer to the summit, the sun started to appear. The group rested together near a cliff, and we got this picture.

## Descent

On the way down, the other trekkers were sliding and we joined in. It was really fun! I almost sprained my knee though because I braked too early. S.S. also went hurdling down at first rotating and rotating. And almost lost his phone in the snow.

The descent to base camp took a while, but all of us eventually made it back.

Back at base camp, S.Y. and I developed headaches and took medicine while we rested. K. bhaiya wanted us to go as far as Juda-ka-Talab, but we didn't want to stay any longer and motivated ourselves to go all the way down to Sankari.

We had already climbed four kilometres and descended four, yet still decided to walk another eight kilometres down to Sankari. K. stayed with those of us at the back so no one got lost.

### New skill unlocked
Suddenly, we saw two locals practically parkour down the hill we had been carefully trying to descend. S.J. and I were astonished by their jumps and footwork. I tried copying their movements—and, somehow, it worked. I made it down the first hill much faster than usual. 

I had unlocked a new skill! Descending quick! Basically I had to gain momentum, and gravity would just pull me down. I just had to carefully place my feet to not lose balance. 

I got far ahead of my group. After seeing me jump around, S.J. tried it too—and it worked for him as well. When he finally caught up, he said it felt like a game had just shown him a “New Skill Unlocked!” notification. The analogy was so funny, lmao. A.P. was too tired to try it, so despite our newfound speed, we regularly had to wait for him.

We eventually returned to Juda-ka-Talab, where S.Y. and the rest of the group were resting. S.J. and I told them about the new skill we had unlocked. S.Y. thought we were fucking around, which gave us the perfect opportunity to demonstrate our profound abilities. We jumped and descended faster than anyone expected, making the group—and N.S.—worry that we would fall flat on our faces. They wanted us to stop, but hell nah. We kept going until the terrain became too rough for confident footing. By then, we were far ahead.

Eventually the trick stopped feeling like a newly unlocked skill and started feeling exhausting, so we returned to an ordinary walk down to Sankari.

We played songs from The Local Train as I got down, along with a doggo that followed me lmao.

## Back in Sankari
Back in Sankari with dead legs, I wanted a shower immediately. The geyser malfunctioned, the electricity vanished the moment I switched it on, and an absurd relay of friends delivered soap and then washing detergent. Eventually, both I and my clothes became clean again.

I put the souvenir rock on my WhatsApp status and received several enthusiastic replies. It was fun catching up on the fest stories I had missed while we were away.

### The rest of the night
We wandered through Sankari's shops in the dark, and I bought a scarf before our final dinner at the stay. Then we collapsed into bed.

# Day 10
## Time to go back
We had to pack everything in the morning. The tea at the stay was already over, so we grabbed some at the shop in front. All my clothes had dried too. Yay!

After tea and Maggi, we lost track of time and the traveler bus arrived before my bag was packed. We rushed to gather everything, and in the chaos I forgot to put my souvenir in my bag. Once packed, we climbed aboard and settled into our seats.

The bus started, and we were off to Dehradun. We'd reach at 8 pm in the evening, after a 10 hour drive.

I thought I had forgotten my souvenir rock, but S.J. had spotted it on the ground and quietly put it in my bag. I felt so happy I could kiss him.

The return ride was easier overall, although someone still felt unwell from the winding roads. We stopped at the same dhaba we had visited on the way up, where I just got a cola.

## Dehradun
Back in Dehradun, we struggled to locate a hotel, but we eventually did. Settled there for a bit. My nose had been shredded due to the cold and the constant rubbing I did with my glove. 

We got mosambi juice and ate at a vegetarian restaurant before returning to the hotel to rest.

The night was cozy, but my legs were suffering.

# Day 11
Since our train back to Delhi left at about 5 am, we woke close to 4:30 and headed straight to the railway station. The train arrived beforehand, and we sat together. I got two Coke Zeros and spent the ride reflecting on the trip.
## Project Hail Mary
We bought tickets to IMAX theatre experience of Project Hail Mary. The train got late however, and we got really really late while getting to the Select City Walk mall in Delhi. For a show of 12:20 pm, we arrived at 13:30 pm. Sigh.

Movie was great however. I had to read the initial plot from Wikipedia to understand anything lmao.

The Delhi mall was lively, crowded, and dramatically more fashionable than what we were used to back home. Everything was expensive: luggage storage cost ₹200 per person, and S.Y. and I somehow spent a fortune on two Baskin-Robbins ice creams. We agreed that no one should know about this shameful act. BRUH.

After the mall, we headed to the railway station and ordered a swiggy. We sat in our train at 6 pm, and before the swiggy dude could arrive, the train was already gone.

With the small amount of packets and chips we still had, we finished it all up and sat in our seats talking about random things. How R.C. kept pooping all the trip and throwing missiles. This missile joke had caught on because on one night in Juda-ka-Talab camp, R.C. had farted a 'poooooh' sound making it ultra hilarious for everyone. And R.C. had also tried pooping close to the summit. We got food through ecatering actually.

The e-catering food arrived at 11 pm, when everyone was tired and mostly asleep. We ate what we could and finally went to sleep.
